00 Understanding Europe

Classical, Faith and Power — Seven Stages of History

A peninsula-continent facing the Atlantic: how did it set out from the Greek city-states and the Roman Empire, pass through the fracture of the Christian world, the rise and fall of several empires, the emergence of nation-states and two world wars, and arrive at today's coexistence of several political forms? The European Union is only one of the present-day arrangements — it does not cover the whole of Europe, and it is not the destination of this history.

The story is organized into seven historical stages, with representative turning points as reading guides. One caution: regions did not change in step, and in any one period different corners of the continent could be in completely different conditions — while Florence was patronizing painters, Balkan peasants were being entered in Ottoman tax registers; while London was beginning to smoke, Russia still had serfdom. So every stage tells two lines at once: one running through Latin Western Europe, and one running through the Byzantine and Orthodox world, the Slavic peoples, Poland–Lithuania, and the Ottoman-ruled southeast.

Before you begin: three senses of "Europe"

Geographical Europe: the landmass stretching from the Atlantic to the Urals. A natural category — whose boundary is itself a human convention.

Cultural Europe: often equated with "Christendom" or the "Latin–Germanic tradition". But Christianity itself first divided into a Latin and a Greek branch, and southeastern Europe lived for centuries under Ottoman rule and deep Islamic influence. Europe was never a single culture, but at least two or three intertwined traditions.

Political Europe: the dynastic realms of the Middle Ages, the sovereign states of the early modern era, multinational empires and today's regional organizations are different political forms — and for a long time they existed side by side. While Western Europe moved toward nation-states, central and eastern Europe was still a world of multinational empires; while some Western European states pooled sovereignty into a union, others were leaving one.

As for the European Union: founded in 1993 (Treaty of Maastricht), it is one cooperative arrangement among some present-day European states. It equals neither geographical nor cultural Europe, and it does not represent a common direction for all European countries — the United Kingdom left in 2020. Treating the EU as the destination of European history is the most common stumbling block in understanding Europe.

The stages below follow the fundamental turns in Europe's political forms: the making of the classical world → the fracture and recomposition of the Christian world → the re-drawing of boundaries → the age of monarchs and empires → revolution, nation and empire → collapse in the two world wars → division, upheaval and reassembly.

One timeline for the whole story

  • 776 BC First recorded Olympic Games (traditional date) Age of the city-states
  • 509 BC Roman Republic founded
  • 27 BC Augustus: Empire begins
  • AD 313 Edict of Milan Christianity legal
  • AD 330 / 395 Constantinople founded / empire divided between East and West
  • AD 476 Western Rome falls (the Eastern Empire lasts nearly a thousand years more)
  • AD 800 Charlemagne crowned
  • AD 862 or so Kievan Rus emerges (traditional date)
  • AD 962 Otto I crowned, Holy Roman Empire
  • AD 1054 East–West Schism
  • AD 1096 First Crusade
  • AD 1204 Fourth Crusade sacks Constantinople
  • AD 1347 Black Death spreads
  • AD 1389 Battle of Kosovo; the Ottomans push deep into the Balkans
  • AD 1453 Constantinople falls; the Byzantine Empire ends
  • AD 1492 Columbus reaches the Americas
  • AD 1517 Luther's Theses Reformation
  • AD 1526 Battle of Mohács; much of Hungary falls to the Ottomans
  • AD 1648 Peace of Westphalia
  • AD 1683 Siege of Vienna; Ottoman expansion in central Europe halted
  • AD 1687 Newton's Principia published
  • AD 1721 Russia proclaimed an empire
  • AD 1772 / 1793 / 1795 Poland partitioned three times
  • AD 1789 French Revolution
  • AD 1815 Congress of Vienna
  • AD 1830 Greek independence
  • 1861 / 1871 Italy and Germany unified
  • AD 1878 Congress of Berlin redraws the Balkans
  • AD 1914 First World War
  • AD 1918 The German, Austro-Hungarian, Russian and Ottoman empires collapse
  • AD 1939 Second World War
  • AD 1945 Second World War ends; Europe divided in two
  • AD 1955 Warsaw Pact founded
  • AD 1968 Prague Spring
  • AD 1989 Revolutions in Eastern Europe; the Berlin Wall falls
  • AD 1991 USSR dissolves
  • AD 2004 Some central and eastern European states join the EU (not all of Europe)
