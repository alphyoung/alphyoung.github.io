## Classical, Faith and Power — Six Stages of History

How did a peninsula-continent facing the Atlantic — setting out from the Greek city-states and the Roman Empire, passing through the integration of Christendom, the fracture of a common faith, the rise of nation-states and two world wars — arrive at the Europe held together today by a union? The story is reorganized into six historical stages, each bounded by a clear turning point: an overview first, then the detail.

## Before you begin: four senses of "Europe"

**Geographical Europe**: the landmass stretching from the Atlantic to the Urals. A natural category — whose boundary is itself a human convention.

**Cultural Europe**: often equated with "Christendom" or the "Latin–Germanic tradition". Yet southeastern Europe lived for centuries under Orthodox and Ottoman influence, and does not share that same narrative.

**Political Europe**: the dynastic realms of the Middle Ages, the sovereign states of the early modern era, today's European Union — these are different political forms. A single family could rule several countries; states can pool part of their sovereignty into a union.

**The European Union**: founded in 1993 (Treaty of Maastricht), a specific contemporary organization. It equals neither geographical nor cultural Europe. Confusing the three is the most common stumbling block in understanding Europe.

The stages below follow the fundamental turns in Europe's political forms: **the making of the classical world → the integration of Latin Christendom → the fracture of a single faith → the maturing of sovereign states → the rise of nation-states → union after war**.
