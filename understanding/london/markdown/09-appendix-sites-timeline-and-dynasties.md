# Appendix — Sites, Timeline and Dynasties: Reading London on the ground

*AD 43 – present*

> Eleven places to read the city, one selected timeline, and the dynasties that ruled it.

## I. Seeing London with its history

**Table 01 · Principal historical sites**

| Site | Main period | What to look for on site |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Remains of the London Wall | Roman and medieval | Tell Roman masonry from later additions; understand the boundary of the old city |
| Tower of London | Norman to modern | The multiple roles of the White Tower, palace, prison and royal ceremony |
| Westminster Abbey | Medieval to present | Coronations, royal burials and national commemoration; do not confuse it with Parliament |
| Palace of Westminster | Medieval, nineteenth century | The difference in date between Westminster Hall and the Gothic Revival new palace |
| Hampton Court Palace | Tudor, Stuart | Henry VIII’s court and the Puritan conference of 1604 |
| Blackfriars | Medieval, Reformation | How a place name preserves the boundary of a vanished monastery |
| Banqueting House, Whitehall | Stuart period (including William III of Orange) | The site Charles I walked out through to the scaffold |
| St Paul’s Cathedral | After 1666 | Rebuilding after the fire, imperial commemoration and wartime London |
| Broad Street pump memorial, Soho | Nineteenth century | How John Snow investigated cholera through spatial distribution |
| West India Docks | From 1802; nineteenth century to present | Sugar, slavery, warehousing and the transformation of the docks |
| Barbican | After the Second World War | Housing and a cultural centre planned on bombed ground |

> **Three questions to ask of any ruin**
>
> Is what you see the original? Which period does it belong to? Is the conclusion based on the building, on archaeology, or on later legend? London often layers Roman, medieval and modern remains on the same spot, and buildings standing next to each other are not therefore the same age.

## III. Table of English and British dynasties

A dynasty is the family a monarch belongs to; it is not the same as the name of a state. Before 1707 this is mainly the history of English dynasties; after 1707 it is Great Britain, and from 1801 the United Kingdom period.

| Dynasty or period | Dates | Representative rulers or institutions | London thread |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Late Anglo-Saxon and Danish kings | 927–1066, alternating with the Danish dynasty | Athelstan, Edward the Confessor, Cnut | England unified; London and Westminster rise in importance |
| Norman dynasty | 1066–1154 | William I, Henry I, Stephen | Tower of London built; Norman rule established |
| Plantagenet main line | 1154–1399 | Henry II, John, Edward I | Common law, Parliament, Magna Carta and the Black Death |
| Lancaster and York (Plantagenet branches) | 1399–1485 | Henry V, Henry VI, Edward IV, Richard III | The Wars of the Roses and London’s political backing |
| Tudor | 1485–1603 | Henry VIII, Mary I, Elizabeth I | Reformation, dissolution of the monasteries and theatre culture |
| Stuart period (including William III of Orange) | 1603–1649, 1660–1714 | James I, Charles I, Charles II, Anne | Civil war, restoration, the Great Fire, the Glorious Revolution and the Union of 1707 |
| Commonwealth and Protectorate | 1649–1660 | Council of State, Oliver Cromwell | The monarchy abolished for a time |
| Hanoverian | 1714–1901 | George I, George III, Victoria | Constitutional monarchy, industrialisation and imperial expansion |
| Saxe-Coburg and Gotha | 1901–1917 | Edward VII, George V | The royal house renamed during the First World War |
| Windsor | 1917–present | George V (from 1917), George VI, Elizabeth II, Charles III | Two world wars, the end of empire and the modern monarchy |
