# Understanding London

Dynasties, religion, city and people along the Thames

> A city history woven from monarchy, commerce, faith and migration

From Iron Age Britons, Roman Londinium and the Anglo-Saxon heptarchy, to the Norman Conquest, the Tudor Reformation, the Renaissance, oceanic trade and the modern plural city — follow the Thames to understand London, and its relationship with Britain and the world.

## First, get the map straight

London had a river before it had a city. The Thames runs east to the sea and west into the interior; bridges, wharves and roads linked the city’s commercial core, its political centre and its port district. London’s centre of gravity has moved too: the Roman city stood where the City of London is today, an Anglo-Saxon trading settlement grew up to the west for a time, and the focus then returned inside the old walls.

Read London by first recognising three extents. The **City of London** is the ancient commercial core of about one square mile. **Westminster** gathers the royal palace, the abbey, Parliament and government. **Greater London** is the metropolitan administrative area whose modern form dates from 1965. The three are connected, but they are not interchangeable.

This volume uses eight chapters of narrative history to link the centuries — from the Roman city, the heptarchy and the Middle Ages through the Reformation, the Renaissance, maritime expansion, the industrial metropolis and the modern immigrant society — with an appendix covering the principal sites, a full timeline and a table of English dynasties. Stories are adapted from historical sources; legend and historical fact are distinguished where they differ.
