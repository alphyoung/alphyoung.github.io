# Understanding London

Dynasties, religion, city and people along the Thames

> A city history woven from monarchy, commerce, faith and migration

From Iron Age Britons, Roman Londinium and the Anglo-Saxon heptarchy, to the Norman Conquest, the Tudor Reformation, the Renaissance, oceanic trade and the modern plural city — follow the Thames to understand London, and its relationship with Britain and the world.

_Reading edition · September 2026_

## First, get the map straight

London had a river before it had a city. The Thames flows east towards the sea and connects London with the interior to the west; bridges, wharves and roads linked the city’s commercial core, its political centre and its port district. London’s centre of gravity has moved too: the Roman city stood where the City of London is today, an Anglo-Saxon trading settlement grew up to the west for a time, and the focus then returned inside the old walls.

Read London by first recognising three extents. The **City of London** is the ancient commercial core of about one square mile. **Westminster** gathers the royal palace, the abbey, Parliament and government. **Greater London** is the metropolitan administrative area whose modern form dates from 1965. The three are connected, but they are not interchangeable.

This volume uses eight thematic chapters to explore the centuries — from the Roman city, the heptarchy and the Middle Ages through the Reformation, the Renaissance, maritime expansion, the industrial metropolis and the modern immigrant society — with an appendix covering the principal sites, a timeline of selected events and a table of English dynasties. Chapters III–V overlap in time, treating religion, culture and maritime trade in parallel. The eighteenth century and 1901–1939 receive only brief connecting references; this is a selective reader, not a continuous, exhaustive history of London. Stories are adapted from historical sources; legend and historical fact are distinguished where they differ.

## The eight chapters

* **Chapter I · River and Rome** — Where London begins (AD 43 – 5th century)
* **Chapter II · Heptarchy and Middle Ages** — From Lundenwic to the Tower of London (5th – 15th century)
* **Chapter III · The Tudor Reformation** — Marriage, church and Puritans (1509 – 1603; Stuart epilogue)
* **Chapter IV · Renaissance London** — Print, humanism and the theatre (1476 – 1600s)
* **Chapter V · The Age of Sail** — Merchantmen, privateers and the beginnings of empire (1497 – 1600s)
* **Chapter VI · Civil War, Fire and Finance** — Politics, buildings and finance reshaped London (1642 – 1694)
* **Chapter VII · The Victorian Era** — How an industrial metropolis worked (1837 – 1901)
* **Chapter VIII · War, Migration and Modern London** — An imperial capital becomes a world city (1940 – present)
