01 Stage 1 Foundations and the Frankish Era

Germany and France both have roots in the Frankish kingdom. The ancient Germanic tribes had no unified state. After the Franks established their kingdom, the Treaty of Verdun divided the empire into three in 843. East Francia became an important foundation for the formation of Germany.

AD 9 Battle of the Teutoburg Forest · 800 Charlemagne crowned emperor · 843 Treaty of Verdun

1.1 Germanic tribes and the Frankish kingdom

Antiquity–800

The ancient Germanic peoples comprised many tribes; there was no unified Germany. The Battle of the Teutoburg Forest in AD 9 checked Roman expansion east of the Rhine, but it cannot be treated as Germany's founding.

The Franks subsequently established a kingdom in western Europe. Clovis of the Merovingian dynasty accepted Catholic Christianity, strengthening the monarchy's ties with the Church and the Catholic, Romanized population. Charlemagne of the Carolingian dynasty expanded the realm further and was crowned emperor by the pope in Rome in 800.

Territory · A comparison with the modern map

Charlemagne's rule extended across present-day France, Belgium, the Netherlands, Luxembourg, western and southern Germany, and northern Italy, among other areas. The modern Franco-German border did not yet exist.

Marriage, conversion and royal power

Clovis's wife Clotilde came from the Burgundian royal family and was Catholic. Their marriage illustrates the connection between dynastic alliances and religion, although Clovis's conversion also had political and social dimensions.

Charlemagne married a daughter of the Lombard king Desiderius, later repudiated the marriage, and conquered the Lombard kingdom in 774. Marriage could temporarily ease relations without eliminating struggles for power.
