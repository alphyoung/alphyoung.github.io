# 07 Appendix Territorial Overview and Sources

## Territorial overview by period

How the boundaries changed, in one table

| Period | Political space | Key to interpreting the boundaries |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Charlemagne's era | Spanned present-day France, much of Germany, including conquered Saxony, the Low Countries, northern Italy and other areas | The modern Franco-German border did not exist |
| East Francia | Centred mainly east of the Rhine, with some western bank territories | Germany formed gradually rather than through a sudden founding event |
| Medieval First Empire | Extensive lands within the German, Italian and Burgundian kingdoms, together with Bohemia | Imperial rights and effective rule did not always coincide |
| Early modern First Empire | Weaker ties to Italy and western territories; internal territorial divisions; separation of Switzerland and the Netherlands confirmed in 1648 | It was not a modern centralized state |
| Habsburg expansion | Family possessions crossed the empire's boundaries | Spain, Hungary and overseas possessions cannot all be counted as German territory |
| German Confederation (1815–1866) | Included Austria, Bohemia and other regions, but not all Austrian or Prussian possessions | Confederation boundaries differed from those of its member kingdoms |
| Second German Empire (1871–1918) | Excluded Austria and Bohemia; included eastern Prussian territories and Alsace-Lorraine | The result of the Lesser German solution |
| Weimar Republic | Postwar contraction; East Prussia separated from the main territory; special arrangements for the Saar and Danzig | Treaties reorganized the European order |
| Nazi period | Annexation of neighbouring territories and occupation of extensive parts of Europe | National territory, protectorates and occupied areas must be distinguished |
| Two postwar German states | Formed within the postwar occupation zones, with temporary special arrangements for Berlin and the Saar | The loss of former eastern territories and the division into two states were separate issues |
| Reunification and settlement, 1990–1992 | GDR accession in 1990; final-settlement and border treaties entered into force in 1991–1992 | It did not restore the old empire's territory |

Marriages connected courts, religion divided political camps, and war and inheritance changed maps. Modern Germany's national boundaries and political legitimacy increasingly came to be defined by treaties, constitutions and civic institutions.

## Reading historical place names

| Historical name | Modern reference | Caution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Königsberg | Kaliningrad, Russia | The city and northern East Prussia came under Soviet control after 1945; the city was renamed in 1946 |
| Danzig | Gdańsk, Poland | Its Free City status after the First World War differed from both annexation and ordinary provincial government |
| Memel | Klaipėda, Lithuania | Distinguish the city from the surrounding territory detached after the First World War |
| Königgrätz / Sadowa | Hradec Králové / Sadová, Czechia | Alternative geographical names associated with the battle of 3 July 1866 |
| Lotharingia | A medieval realm including areas now in several countries | Larger than the later French region of Lorraine; not interchangeable with it |
| Prussia | A duchy, later a monarchy with widely dispersed lands | East Prussia, the Kingdom of Prussia and all German territory are different concepts |

## Source method

Treaty texts establish what was agreed; ratification records establish when it took effect. Museum and scholarly biographies help distinguish family relationships and royal titles. Maps are dated interpretations and must be checked against their legends and source notes. Disputed images or dates are not treated as conclusive evidence. Further institutions used in this edition include the [German Historical Museum](https://www.dhm.de/lemo/), [House of the History of the Federal Republic](https://www.hdg.de/lemo/), [Deutsche Biographie](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/), [German History in Documents and Images](https://germanhistorydocs.org/), [United States Holocaust Memorial Museum](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/) and [House of the Wannsee Conference](https://www.ghwk.de/). Specific supporting pages are linked beside the relevant passages.
