# 07 Appendix Territorial Overview and Sources

## Territorial overview by period

How the boundaries changed, in one table

| Period | Political space | Key to interpreting the boundaries |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Charlemagne's era | Spanned present-day France, southwestern Germany, the Low Countries, northern Italy and other areas | The modern Franco-German border did not exist |
| East Francia | Centred on duchies east of the Rhine | Germany formed gradually rather than through a sudden founding event |
| Medieval First Empire | Extensive lands within the German, Italian and Burgundian kingdoms, together with Bohemia | Imperial rights and effective rule did not always coincide |
| Early modern First Empire | Weaker ties to Italy and western territories; internal territorial divisions; separation of Switzerland and the Netherlands confirmed in 1648 | It was not a modern centralized state |
| Habsburg expansion | Family possessions crossed the empire's boundaries | Spain, Hungary and overseas possessions cannot all be counted as German territory |
| German Confederation (1815–1866) | Included Austria, Bohemia and other regions, but not all Austrian or Prussian possessions | Confederation boundaries differed from those of its member kingdoms |
| Second German Empire (1871–1918) | Excluded Austria and Bohemia; included eastern Prussian territories and Alsace-Lorraine | The result of the Lesser German solution |
| Weimar Republic | Postwar contraction; East Prussia separated from the main territory; special arrangements for the Saar and Danzig | Treaties reorganized the European order |
| Nazi period | Annexation of neighbouring territories and occupation of extensive parts of Europe | National territory, protectorates and occupied areas must be distinguished |
| Two postwar German states | Formed within the postwar occupation zones, with temporary special arrangements for Berlin and the Saar | The loss of former eastern territories and the division into two states were separate issues |
| Reunification in 1990 | United the two postwar German states and confirmed final borders | It did not restore the old empire's territory |

Marriages connected courts, religion divided political camps, and war and inheritance changed maps. Modern Germany's national boundaries and political legitimacy increasingly came to be defined by treaties, constitutions and civic institutions.
