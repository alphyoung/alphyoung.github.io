# 05 Stage 5 Division, Reconstruction and Reunification

After the war, Germany's eastern border shifted westward and its population underwent immense upheaval. Occupation zones gave way to two German states. After the Berlin Wall, Ostpolitik and a peaceful revolution, Germany reunified on 3 October 1990, and treaties definitively confirmed its present borders.

**1949** Two German states established · **1961** Berlin Wall · **1989** Wall opened · **1990** Reunification

## 5.1 Postwar borders and occupation

1945–1949

After 1945, Austria was re-established as an independent state. Most former German territories east of the Oder-Neisse line were placed under Polish administration, while the northern part of East Prussia came under Soviet control. Final legal confirmation followed through subsequent treaties.

### Territory · Germany's eastern border moves west

The changes affected most of Silesia, eastern Pomerania, East Prussia and other areas. Large numbers of German inhabitants fled or were expelled during the final stages of the war and its aftermath, fundamentally transforming the regions' population structure.

The remaining territory was divided into American, British, French and Soviet occupation zones, while Berlin was separately divided into four sectors. The Nuremberg trials held some Nazi leaders accountable for war crimes, crimes against humanity and other offences.

## 5.2 Two German states

1949–1989

| State | Politics and economy | Society and religion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Federal Republic of Germany (West Germany, established 1949) | Federalism, parliamentary democracy and a social market economy; participation in western European integration | Christian Democratic politics encouraged cooperation between some Catholics and Protestants; democratic reconstruction and reflection on the past gradually deepened |
| German Democratic Republic (East Germany, established 1949) | Socialist Unity Party dominance, a planned economy and Soviet influence | Society became increasingly secular; some Protestant churches provided limited space for peace, human-rights and opposition movements |

{% step %}
### 1953

East German workers' protests developed into a widespread uprising, suppressed by Soviet troops and other forces.
{% endstep %}

{% step %}
### 1957

The Saar, which had held a special relationship with France after the war, joined the Federal Republic.
{% endstep %}

{% step %}
### 1961

The Berlin Wall was built. West Berlin had a special status; it was not an ordinary West German federal state.
{% endstep %}

{% step %}
### 1970s

The Brandt government's **Ostpolitik**, or eastern policy, improved relations with East Germany and Eastern European countries.
{% endstep %}

{% step %}
### 9 November 1989

Amid public demonstrations, population outflows and a changing international situation, the Berlin Wall opened.
{% endstep %}

{% step %}
### 3 October 1990

The five re-established states of East Germany joined the Federal Republic, Berlin was reunited, and national reunification took place.
{% endstep %}
{% endstepper %}

{% hint style="info" %}
**Reunification in 1990 did not restore the borders of 1871 or 1937.** The Two Plus Four settlement and the German-Polish Border Treaty confirmed the final boundaries, definitively establishing the Oder-Neisse line as the German-Polish border.
{% endhint %}

Unified Germany continued to address economic differences between east and west and the challenges of social integration. Reflection on Nazi crimes deepened through trials, research, education and remembrance, alongside institutions designed to safeguard human dignity and limit state power.
