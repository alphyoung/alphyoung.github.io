# 09 Appendix Chronology and Date Relationships

This table links events that are easily collapsed into one date. It is an index to the main chapters, not a claim that every transition happened instantly. Years separated by a slash refer to distinct events; a range can indicate a process or a conventional period.

## Frankish and medieval foundations

| Date or sequence | Event and relationship |
| --- | --- |
| AD 9 → AD 14–16 | Defeat of Varus's legions; later Roman campaigns under Germanicus show that the first battle did not immediately end Roman operations beyond the Rhine |
| 751 → 768 → 800 | Carolingians acquire the Frankish kingship; Charlemagne becomes a Frankish king; imperial coronation in Rome on 25 December 800 |
| 814 → 840 → 843 | Charlemagne dies; Louis the Pious dies; the latter's sons agree the division at Verdun after civil war |
| 855 → 870 → 880 | Middle Francia fragments; Meerssen and Ribemont reshape Lotharingia, not all the lands that had belonged to Middle Francia |
| 911 → 919 | End of East Francia's Carolingian kings; Conrad I is elected in 911, followed by Henry I in 919 |
| 936 → 951 → 962 | Otto I's kingship at Aachen; Italian kingship and marriage to Adelaide; imperial coronation at Rome on 2 February 962 |
| 955 | Otto defeats the Magyars at Lechfeld near Augsburg, before his imperial coronation |
| 972 | Otto II marries Theophanu in Rome; this does not merge the western and Byzantine empires |
| 1024 → 1032–1034 | Salian kingship begins with Conrad II; Burgundy's disputed inheritance, coronation and consolidation occur later |
| 1077 → 1122 | Canossa and the Concordat of Worms are separate stages of the Investiture Controversy, not a single final victory of pope or emperor |
| 1138 → 1186 → 1194 | Hohenstaufen kingship begins; Henry VI marries Constance; he obtains the Sicilian crown through claim and conquest |
| 1198 → 1212 | Ottokar I's Bohemian kingship and its hereditary confirmation in the Golden Bull of Sicily |
| c. 1250/1254–1273 | The Great Interregnum: a conventional period of rival claims and weak central kingship, not the absence of all kings |
| 1273 → 1278 | Rudolf I is elected king, then defeats Ottokar II at the Marchfeld; Rudolf is not crowned emperor |
| 1356 | The Golden Bull defines the seven-elector arrangement; Austria is not one of the seven |
| 1438 → 1440 → 1452 | Albert II's election as king; Frederick's royal election; Frederick III's imperial coronation in Rome |
| 1477 → 1496 | Maximilian and Mary of Burgundy marry; Philip the Handsome and Joanna marry in the following generation |
| 1495 | Imperial reform at Worms: common legal institutions develop within the empire rather than replacing it with a centralized nation state |

Sources and discussion: [Frankish foundations](01-stage-1-foundations-and-the-frankish-era.md) and [the medieval and early modern empire](02-stage-2-the-holy-roman-empire.md).

## Reformation, dynastic politics and unification

| Date or sequence | Event and relationship |
| --- | --- |
| 1517 | Luther circulates the Ninety-five Theses; the Reformation unfolds over decades, with earlier reform traditions and multiple centres |
| 1519 → 1520 → 1530 | Charles V's election, royal coronation at Aachen and papal imperial coronation at Bologna are distinct events |
| 1521–1522 → 1526 | Ferdinand takes over Austrian hereditary lands; Bohemian and Hungarian succession claims follow the death of Louis II at Mohács |
| 1555 → 1556 → 1558 | Peace of Augsburg and Charles's transfer of the Netherlands in 1555; Spanish succession and imperial renunciation in 1556; recognition of Ferdinand's imperial succession in 1558 |
| 1618 → 1619 → 1620 | Bohemian revolt begins the Thirty Years' War; Frederick V accepts the Bohemian crown; defeat at White Mountain ends his short reign |
| 1623 → 1648 | Palatine electoral status transferred to Bavaria; a new Palatine electoral seat created in the peace settlement |
| 1648 → 1653 | Westphalian settlement awards Brandenburg Pomeranian territory; subsequent agreements implement the division |
| 1618 → 1657/1660 → 1701 | Brandenburg inherits the Prussian duchy; sovereignty from the Polish Crown is secured and confirmed; Frederick becomes “King in Prussia” on 18 January 1701 |
| 1740–1748 / 1756–1763 | War of the Austrian Succession / Seven Years' War; related stages of Austrian-Prussian rivalry, not one continuous war |
| 1803 → 1804 → 1805 → 1806 | Secularization and territorial reorganization; Austrian imperial title created; Austerlitz; Confederation of the Rhine and dissolution of the Holy Roman Empire |
| 1813 → 1814–1815 → 8 June 1815 | Leipzig defeats Napoleon; Congress of Vienna; German Federal Act establishes the German Confederation |
| 1 January 1834 | Zollverein takes effect; customs integration does not itself create a nation state |
| 18 May 1848 → March–April 1849 | Frankfurt National Assembly convenes; constitution and offer of the crown; Frederick William IV rejects the proposed settlement |
| 1864 → 1866 → 1 July 1867 | War against Denmark; Austro-Prussian War; constitution of the North German federal state takes effect |
| 1 January → 18 January → 4 May 1871 | New federal constitutional arrangements; Versailles proclamation; revised imperial constitution takes effect |

Sources and discussion: [The Holy Roman Empire](02-stage-2-the-holy-roman-empire.md), [unification](03-stage-3-the-second-empire.md) and [royal connections](08-dynasties-and-states.md).

## War, dictatorship and the postwar settlement

| Date or sequence | Event and relationship |
| --- | --- |
| June–August 1914 | Sarajevo assassination is followed by the July Crisis, declarations of war and invasions; political decisions connect these stages |
| 9 → 11 → 28 November 1918 | Abdication announced and republic proclaimed; armistice takes effect; Wilhelm II signs formal abdication |
| 19 January → 31 July → 11/14 August 1919 | National election; constitution adopted; signed and then promulgated into force |
| 28 June 1919 → 10 January 1920 | Versailles Treaty signed, then enters into force; local border arrangements follow different schedules |
| 30 January → 28 February → 23/24 March 1933 | Hitler appointed; emergency rights suspended; Enabling Act passed and promulgated |
| 1938 → March 1939 → September 1939 | Annexation of Austria and Sudetenland; destruction of the remaining Czech state and seizure of Memel; invasion of Poland and European war |
| 22 June 1941 → 20 January 1942 | Invasion of the Soviet Union and expansion of mass murder; Wannsee coordinates an already developing extermination policy |
| 7 → 8/9 May → 5 June 1945 | Reims surrender signed; surrender effective and further signing at Berlin-Karlshorst; Allies assume supreme authority |
| June 1948 → May 1949 | Currency reform and Berlin blockade; blockade lifted after the airlift sustains West Berlin |
| 23 May / 7 October 1949 | Founding dates of the Federal Republic / GDR; the two states do not begin on the same day |
| 1955 | West Germany gains extensive sovereignty and joins NATO; the GDR joins the Warsaw Pact; four-power reservations over Germany remain |
| 1 January 1957 → 6 July 1959 | Saar's political accession, then economic and currency integration |
| 13 August 1961 | Closure of the Berlin border and construction of the Wall begin; distinct from the 1948–1949 blockade |
| 1970 → 1972 → 1973 | Eastern treaties; Basic Treaty signed; Basic Treaty effective and both German states admitted to the UN |
| 9 October → 9 November 1989 | Peaceful Leipzig demonstration; border crossings open in Berlin; neither date is formal reunification |
| March → July → August 1990 | Free GDR election; monetary union; accession vote and Unification Treaty signature |
| 12 September → 3 October → 14 November 1990 | Two Plus Four Treaty signed; reunification; German-Polish Border Treaty signed |
| 15 March 1991 / 16 January 1992 | Two Plus Four Treaty / German-Polish Border Treaty enter into force |

Sources and discussion: [War and dictatorship](04-stage-4-war-collapse-and-nazi-rule.md) and [division and reunification](05-stage-5-division-and-reunification.md). The dates of ratification and treaty effectiveness are particularly important: a signature alone does not explain the whole institutional transition.
