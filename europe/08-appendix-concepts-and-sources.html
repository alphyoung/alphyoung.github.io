08 Appendix Concepts and Sources

The stages, concepts and entities that recur throughout are gathered here as reference tables. Half the value of history lies in knowing the facts; the other half lies in knowing where these concepts come from and where their boundaries are.

A.1 The six stages at a glance

Stage Span Theme in one sentence
Ⅰ Classical foundations 800 BC — AD 476 Greece, Rome and Christianity converge in the Mediterranean
Ⅱ Middle Ages 476 — 1453 Christendom integrates while politics stays dispersed
Ⅲ Fracture of unity 1453 — 1648 Religious unity breaks; the sovereign-state system is established
Ⅳ Century of monarchs 1648 — 1789 Absolute power peaks; science and Enlightenment rise
Ⅴ Revolution and nation-states 1789 — 1914 Popular sovereignty plus industrialization; nation-states take shape
Ⅵ War and integration 1914 — present Two world wars and the Cold War lead toward union

A.2 Key concepts

Concept Meaning Common misconception
City-state (polis) A political unit constituted by a community of citizens Mistaken for a modern state; its citizen body was in fact extremely narrow
Republic A regime constraining power through a senate, elections and fixed terms The assumption that the Roman Republic was democracy for all
Feudalism A hierarchy of relationships maintained by land grants and allegiance Pictured as a tidy bureaucratic pyramid
Sovereignty The supreme power of a state within its territory The assumption that it is ancient; it is a modern product
Nation-state A state whose legitimacy rests on national identity Believing that "one people, one state" is a natural condition
Balance of power Preventing any one power from dominating, and so preserving order Assuming that balance necessarily brings peace
Integration States exercising part of their sovereignty through common institutions Assuming it means creating a single "European state"

A.3 Major political entities in sequence

Polity Approximate dates Key thread
Greek city-states 800 — 146 BC The source of citizen politics, philosophy and drama
Roman Republic / Empire 509 BC — AD 476 (West) Law, provinces and Mediterranean unity
Byzantine Empire 395 — 1453 Orthodoxy, Greek, a thousand years of continuity
Carolingian Empire 800 — partition 843 The first large-scale integration of the West
Holy Roman Empire 962 — 1806 A political framework of many kingdoms, not one nation-state
Ottoman Empire (European lands) 14th century — 1922 Long rule over the Balkans and southeastern Europe
European Union 1993 — present A regional integration experiment based on pooled sovereignty
